RACE MODULE — MARATHON
======================

WHAT THE RACE DEMANDS
---------------------
Marathon readiness rests on aerobic capacity, durability, and race-specific skill
(pacing, fueling, mental execution). For most athletes DURABILITY is the primary
limiter — the runner with a fast 10K who unravels past 30 km lacks durability, not
speed. Marathon fitness is built through consistent aerobic volume and progressive
long-run exposure; speed work and race-pace practice are refinements on a durable
base, never substitutes for it.

DISTANCE-SPECIFIC STRUCTURE
---------------------------
- The long run is the foundation. Marathon-ready peak: 30–35 km (or ~2.5–3 hours);
  28 km is the minimum credible peak. Several runs ≥24 km beat one maximal effort.
  Floors once established: ≥14 km after week 4, ≥18 km after week 6, recovery
  weeks included. If the timeline can't safely reach ≥28 km, say so in the
  overview, frame the race as completion-focused, and progress as far as is safe.
- A midweek medium-long run (14–20 km easy; may start 10–12 km and grow) is
  mandatory in loading weeks and reduces proportionally in recovery weeks.
- Marathon-pace work is a skill earned by durability, not a default. Introduce it
  only when volume and long-run evidence support it (roughly: ≥50–55 km/week with
  a 20–24 km long run, or 6+ consistent weeks without durability concerns) — and
  progress it: brief segments early, 3–6 km blocks mid-plan, extended 8–14 km
  segments inside peak long runs, brief confidence touches in taper. MP work
  replaces a quality session, never adds one. First-time marathoners get minimal
  or none. A durability-limited marathon plan legitimately contains very little MP
  running — justify its presence or absence in the overview.
- Quality tiers by volume: <40 km/week → one quality session, no MP work, 85%+
  easy; 40–60 → up to two, threshold and steady before speed; >60 → full range,
  still max two.
- Fueling practice belongs in long runs beyond ~2 hours, expressed as a practice
  range, and matters more as the long run grows.
- Strength emphasis: posterior chain, hip stability, single-leg durability, core
  endurance for late-race form.

TAPER
-----
2–3 weeks. Final long run ~2 weeks out (moderate, 18–22 km); final quality touch
10–12 days out; race-week volume 40–50% below peak with 3–4 short easy runs,
optional strides two days before, and no gym in the final 10–14 days.

PACE TABLE ROWS (all required)
------------------------------
PACE TABLE:
Recovery: X:XX/km
Easy: X:XX/km
Steady: X:XX/km
Marathon Pace (goal): X:XX/km
Adjusted Training Pace: X:XX/km (only if goal pace exceeds current fitness)
Threshold: X:XX/km
Interval: X:XX/km
Repetition: X:XX/km

Derivation anchors: MP = goal time ÷ 42.195 (locked to goal); threshold ≈ MP −
15–25 sec/km; easy ≈ threshold + 60–90 sec/km; recovery ≈ easy + 15–30 sec/km;
steady between easy and MP; interval ≈ threshold − 10–15 sec/km; repetition ≈
interval − 5–10 sec/km. When goal pace outruns fitness, train off the Adjusted
Training Pace and say so.
