RACE MODULE — ULTRA (50K AND BEYOND)
====================================

WHAT THE RACE DEMANDS
---------------------
Ultra readiness is not weekly mileage — it is time-on-feet adaptation, cumulative
fatigue resistance, fueling and hydration execution under stress, mental
durability, and terrain-specific preparation. Ultras are trained by DURATION and
cumulative fatigue. A plan that builds mileage without building long-run duration
produces a runner who can run a lot, not a runner who can run long. Long runs are
measured in hours as much as kilometres; every run beyond ~2.5–3 hours carries
fueling practice notes; effort (RPE) governs where terrain makes pace unreliable.

DISTANCE-SPECIFIC STRUCTURE
---------------------------
- Peak long-run targets by goal: 50K → 32–40 km or 3.5–5 h, multiple runs ≥28 km.
  100K → 45–55 km or 5–7 h, multiple ≥35 km, at least one ≥6 h effort. 100 miles →
  50–65 km or 6–8 h, multiple ≥45 km, at least one ≥8 h. If the timeline can't
  reach these safely, say so plainly in the overview.
- Back-to-back long weekends (both days scheduled explicitly as long runs) are
  mandatory in peak phases for 100K+ (optional/introductory for first-time 50K):
  appearing in at least 3 build/peak weeks, with no gym Friday–Sunday those weeks.
  Order the days by the athlete's limiter — depletion-first for durability
  builders, longer-second for experienced racers simulating late-race fatigue.
- A midweek medium-long run (15–25 km, 1.5–3 h easy) sustains cumulative fatigue
  adaptation.
- Recovery weeks keep a real long run (50K: 18–24 km; 100K: 22–30 km; 100M:
  25–35 km) — ultra adaptation requires sustained exposure; recovery comes from
  reduced intensity and total volume, not from deleting the long run.
- Quality is secondary: threshold/steady work in modest doses, capped by the
  contract; under 50 km/week → one quality session and 85%+ easy.
- Terrain adaptation: road → reliable pace targets and even rhythm; trail →
  effort-first pacing and elevation targets on key long runs; mountain → pace
  targets illustrative only, mandatory hiking segments in long runs, weekly
  elevation gain tracked alongside distance, eccentric-loading strength emphasis.
- 100-mile extras: night-running exposure on 2–3 long runs, real-food fueling
  practice beyond 5 hours, programmed walk/run ratios on 4+ hour efforts, race
  execution and contingency review in the final two weeks.
- Strength emphasis: posterior chain, single-leg strength, eccentric loading for
  descents, power-hiking musculature for mountain events.

TAPER
-----
50K: 10–14 days. 100K and beyond: 2–3 weeks. Final significant long run ~2 weeks
out at moderate duration; final-week volume down 40–60%; race week is short easy
running, generous rest, and logistics/mental rehearsal rather than fitness.
