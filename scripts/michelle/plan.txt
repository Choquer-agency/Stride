COACHING OVERVIEW
You arrive with a real aerobic engine from consistent Peloton work — what you don't have yet is running-specific durability: tendons, joints, and the ability to hold a conversational pace without stopping, and that is your primary limiter. You've told me you're ready to run from day one, so every session is continuous running with a rough pace guide — do your best to stay in the given range, and if you ever need a brief walk, take it and settle straight back into the run, no judgment. The build is deliberately patient: short runs that grow in duration week over week until your long run passes two hours before race week, with the pace guide tightening slightly as you get stronger. Running the full 21.1 km without a single walk break is an achievable goal on this timeline, provided you respect the boring weeks as much as the milestone weeks. Every session lives on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday as committed; Thursdays are Peloton Zone 2 rides for the first six weeks to let your legs adapt before the third run day arrives in week 7, and from week 25 a short fourth session appears on Wednesdays because at your peak long runs it materially protects the goal. One rule is non-negotiable: any pain that changes how you walk or is still there the next morning means rest that day — never run through it, and never compress a missed week into the next one. Monday stays yours: yoga, and a hard rest day from running.

EFFORT TABLE:
Easy Run: 8:15–9:00/km early, easing to 7:30–8:15/km by race season — conversational, Zone 2 heart rate, RPE 3–4
Long Run: same effort, sit at the easier end of your current range
Ride (Zone 2): steady, could chat the whole ride, RPE 3–4
